  1. 1853

    The Montgomery Block, the city's first fireproof office building, was put up on this site by Henry Wager Halleck.

    NoeHill in San Francisco
  2. May 14, 1856

    James King of William, editor of the Evening Bulletin, died in the Montgomery Block after being shot by James Casey.

    NoeHill in San Francisco
  3. 1959

    The Montgomery Block, which had come through the 1906 earthquake and fire, was demolished and the site became a parking lot.

    NoeHill in San Francisco
  4. 1969

    The Transamerica Pyramid, designed by William L. Pereira & Associates from 1969 and finished in 1971-72, with Redwood Park laid out by Tom Galli.

    Modern Architecture and Landscape Design Context Statement, adopted 2011
  5. June 26, 1969

    On discretionary review, the City Planning Commission approved, 5 to 2 and against its staff's recommendation, the building permit for the Transamerica Corporation's 853-foot pyramid-shaped headquarters tower, with 510,000 square feet of floor area over three levels of underground parking.

    San Francisco City Planning Commission minutes, San Francisco Public Library (Internet Archive)
  6. August 7, 1969

    The City Planning Commission approved a three-level underground parking garage of about 280 stalls (up to 340 cars with attendant parking) for the proposed Transamerica headquarters office building, in a C-3-O district.

    San Francisco City Planning Commission minutes, San Francisco Public Library (Internet Archive)
  7. November 13, 1969

    On discretionary review the City Planning Commission voted 4-3 to approve revised plans for the Transamerica Building, with a central core, a street-level promenade, a bank on the first floor and a restaurant and shops on the second.

    San Francisco City Planning Commission minutes, San Francisco Public Library (Internet Archive)
  8. December 4, 1969

    The City Planning Commission voted 5-1 to approve a modified parking garage of 251 spaces for the Transamerica headquarters building, on a site enlarged by part of Lot 8 and a further strip of Merchant Street.

    San Francisco City Planning Commission minutes, San Francisco Public Library (Internet Archive)
  9. April 16, 1970

    The City Planning Commission authorized the Director of Planning to approve revised plans for the Transamerica Corporation office building, which reduced its floor area by about 60,000 square feet, enlarged the open plaza under it and specified bronze glass.

    San Francisco City Planning Commission minutes, San Francisco Public Library (Internet Archive)

Modern & Postmodern Architectural Styles Historic Context Statement (2024)

StyleCorporate Late Modernism
Architect as surveyedWilliam L. Pereira & Associates; Gin Wong, design architect
Year built as surveyed1972

The Transamerica Pyramid, designed by William L. Pereira & Associates with Gin Wong as design architect, unveiled in 1969 and completed in 1972. A touchstone of the anti-Manhattanization argument and now read as one of the city's defining examples of Corporate Late Modernism, with highly repetitive fenestration and precast panel cladding. Redwood Park, the landscape designed by Anthony Guzzardo beside the Transamerica Pyramid, offered as a concession for approval of the tower and an early example of the trade of public open space for downtown height and bulk.

Earlier record

  1. 1964

    The San Francisco Mountain Springs Water Company bought the brewery; the sculptor who had restored it kept a right of residency until 1975.

    India Basin Survey and Context Statement, adopted 2008
  2. 1938

    The buildings had gone to ruin by the time Adrien Voisin bought them and restored them as a residence and art studio. The brewery building and the tunnels are still standing.

    Area B Survey context statement, 2010
  3. 1890–1919

    After Burnell's death in 1890 his widow, brother and several nephews ran the brewery, until Prohibition took effect in 1919.

    Area B Survey context statement, 2010
  4. 1870

    John Hamlin Burnell, an English immigrant born at East Hoathly, Sussex in 1849 who reached San Francisco by way of Vancouver in the late 1860s, founded the Albion Ale and Porter Brewery to make beer in the English tradition. He had found the fresh water he needed in a natural outfall at the corner of 9th (Innes) Avenue and G (Griffith) Street, bought a 75-by-100-foot lot, drove tunnels deep into the hillside to increase the output, and built above them a brewery of imported limestone and local float rock hewn from Candlestick Hill. The plant also held fermenting kettles and malt mills.

    Area B Survey context statement, 2010

The 1899–1900 Sanborn maps give the brewery's address as 837–39 9th Avenue South, the earlier name of Innes Avenue. The assessor dates the building 1920; the 2010 survey dates it 1870.

Recorded as the Albion Brewery, common name Albion Castle: a three-story stone tower of native serpentine laid in random courses with dressed masonry quoins, a pyramidal roof capped by a ventilator, and a one-story west wing that is part of the historic structure. Built as a brewery and used as a residence since 1938. This survey has the brewer buying three lots on the south side of 9th (Innes) Avenue from the South San Francisco Homestead & Railroad Association and commissioning English-born masons to lay the brew house up out of local "float rock" and possibly imported ballast stones, hewn from Bayview Hill; the Area B survey has one 75-by-100-foot lot and Candlestick Hill. It gives the designation as City Landmark No. 60 in April 1974, the only city landmark in the survey area; this is also the only India Basin building the 1968 Here Today survey listed, and one of two on which San Francisco Architectural Heritage holds a file.

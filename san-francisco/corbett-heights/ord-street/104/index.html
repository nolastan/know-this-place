  1. August 1905

    Offered for sale at $6,000, when the building was half its present width: three flats of six, six and seven rooms with bath on a lot 25 by 137.6 feet, let for $55 a month. A second notice that September put the cost of the building alone at $7,000.

    Corbett Heights Neighbors, "May Neighborhood News", Vol. VIII, No. 5 (1 May 2026)
  2. December 1, 1906

    Two permits for a separate house on the 16-foot strip beside the building came to nothing: this one for a single-family dwelling 16 by 36 feet and 25 feet high, withdrawn the following April, and one of 28 August 1907 for a two-family dwelling 16 by 136 feet and 30 feet high. The strip was absorbed into the building instead.

    Corbett Heights Neighbors, "May Neighborhood News", Vol. VIII, No. 5 (1 May 2026)
  3. 1908

    The addition was designed and built by N. W. Mohr, who matched the window trim of the 1880s facade; the bay windows on the 17th Street side are of the later design, and the front is not quite symmetrical as a result.

    Corbett Heights Neighbors, "May Neighborhood News", Vol. VIII, No. 5 (1 May 2026)
  4. August 1, 1908

    A permit for two flats and a store added to the three-storey frame building, at a stated cost of $3,900, extending 84 feet along the 16-foot side lot to a height of 43 feet, with Lattig Construction Company as builder. The store took the address 100 Ord Street, and the numbers 104, 106 and 108 Ord and 4301-4303-4305 17th Street date from the work.

    Corbett Heights Neighbors, "May Neighborhood News", Vol. VIII, No. 5 (1 May 2026)
  5. 1919

    Fire-insurance maps show the completed addition, the store, and the building at twice its earlier width, divided into six flats.

    Corbett Heights Neighbors, "May Neighborhood News", Vol. VIII, No. 5 (1 May 2026)
  6. April 20, 1920

    A permit turned the grocery store at 100 Ord into a three-room apartment with a bath and put a garage in the basement with its own entrance on Ord Street, at a stated cost of $750. The converted space became 104 Ord.

    Corbett Heights Neighbors, "May Neighborhood News", Vol. VIII, No. 5 (1 May 2026)
  7. December 15, 1921

    A revised permit rearranged the storerooms into a two-room apartment and a garage at a stated cost of $1,450, with N. W. Mohr as architect and W. F. Lovell as contractor; 120 yards of earth came out for the garage.

    Corbett Heights Neighbors, "May Neighborhood News", Vol. VIII, No. 5 (1 May 2026)
  8. 1931

    Remodelled by the contractors A & B Quinn.

    Corbett Heights Neighbors, "May Neighborhood News", Vol. VIII, No. 5 (1 May 2026)
  9. 1940

    Permits to repair the posts, piers and stairways.

    Corbett Heights Neighbors, "May Neighborhood News", Vol. VIII, No. 5 (1 May 2026)
  10. 1975

    The house and its interior, and the interior of 4301 17th Street, appear in an episode of The Streets of San Francisco, along with the park across the road.

    Corbett Heights Neighbors, "May Neighborhood News", Vol. VIII, No. 5 (1 May 2026)
  11. 1976

    Rated A as a historic resource, and scored a 3 on almost every measure of that year's architectural survey, from facade proportions and detailing to its contribution to the streetscape. It also appears in the 1968 Here Today appendix.

    Corbett Heights Neighbors, "May Neighborhood News", Vol. VIII, No. 5 (1 May 2026)

The 1976 city architectural survey rated buildings 0 to 5, 5 highest, and was never adopted as a register of historic resources.

  1. 1935–1941

    The Works Progress Administration built a new playground, fieldhouse, convenience station and tennis courts in the former quarry here — a pair of tennis courts and a convenience station near 15th and Beaver streets.

    San Francisco New Deal Historic Context Statement, adopted 2023
  2. 1936

    City purchases the Corona Heights lot

    The San Francisco Board of Supervisors purchased the Corona Heights lot for $27,333.

    Lisa Dunseth, “The Junior Recreation Museum in Balboa Park: The Brainchild of Josephine Randall and Bert Walker,” The Argonaut: Journal of the San Francisco Historical Society, vol. 32, no. 1 (Summer 2021), pp. 64–80
  3. 1941

    The Recreation Commission set aside the Corona Heights site for the museum.

    Lisa Dunseth, “The Junior Recreation Museum in Balboa Park: The Brainchild of Josephine Randall and Bert Walker,” The Argonaut: Journal of the San Francisco Historical Society, vol. 32, no. 1 (Summer 2021), pp. 64–80
  4. 1947

    San Francisco voters passed a $12 million bond measure to fund a new Junior Museum building.

    Lisa Dunseth, “The Junior Recreation Museum in Balboa Park: The Brainchild of Josephine Randall and Bert Walker,” The Argonaut: Journal of the San Francisco Historical Society, vol. 32, no. 1 (Summer 2021), pp. 64–80
  5. September 1949

    The $12 million bond measure's funds were finally appropriated, and construction of the new museum building began.

    Lisa Dunseth, “The Junior Recreation Museum in Balboa Park: The Brainchild of Josephine Randall and Bert Walker,” The Argonaut: Journal of the San Francisco Historical Society, vol. 32, no. 1 (Summer 2021), pp. 64–80
  6. September 23, 1951

    Junior museum opens at Corona Heights

    The Josephine D. Randall Junior Museum opened at Corona Heights, dedicated in honor of Josephine Randall, the city's superintendent of recreation for twenty-five years; the Recreation and Park Commission suspended its rule against naming buildings for living people to do so.

    Lisa Dunseth, “The Junior Recreation Museum in Balboa Park: The Brainchild of Josephine Randall and Bert Walker,” The Argonaut: Journal of the San Francisco Historical Society, vol. 32, no. 1 (Summer 2021), pp. 64–80

  1. 1912

    Designed by Ingleside Terraces developer and architect Joseph Leonard as his own residence; designated San Francisco Landmark #213.

    OMI Neighborhoods Historical Context Statement, adopted 2010
  2. 1912

    Willard E. Worden photographed the house.

    Willard E. Worden Glass Plate Negatives (SFP 22), San Francisco Public Library
  3. 1950s

    Later home to Assistant District Attorney Cecil Poole, who integrated Ingleside Terraces.

    OMI Neighborhoods Historical Context Statement, adopted 2010
  4. 1958

    The first African American household in Ingleside Terraces moved in here in 1958, into a tract the developer Joseph Leonard had built for white buyers only; a cross was burned in front of the house shortly afterwards. The building is a designated city landmark.

    African American Citywide Historic Context Statement, adopted 2024

Year built as surveyed1911

The Joseph Leonard-Cecil F. Poole House built in 1911 to designs by Joseph Leonard — an excellent Craftsman house with wide overhanging eaves, exposed rafter ends and roof beams, triangular knee braces, ornamental grillwork at the gables, stone in the prominent exterior chimney, an exposed foundation, a continuous row of transomed windows and grey shingles.

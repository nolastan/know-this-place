  1. July 15, 1900

    The San Francisco and San Mateo Electric Railroad broke ground for its Geneva Avenue Shops at San Jose and Geneva avenues. The brick complex was to hold four buildings: a two-storey office with a single-storey transformer room attached to it, a twenty-track car shed and a blacksmith's shop.

    San Francisco Planning Department, Geneva Office Building and Power House Demolition Project: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 97.345E, 1998.
  2. 1901

    Built 1901 as the San Francisco and San Mateo Railway's company car house and office building at San Jose and Geneva avenues; by the 1920s it housed more streetcars than any other barn in the city's electric-car era.

    OMI Neighborhoods Historical Context Statement, adopted 2010
  3. April 22, 1901

    The shops opened for business; the power house was finished later in the year. The office stands 45 feet high and about 122 feet by 47, wood-framed with unreinforced brick walls inside and out, late Romanesque Revival with Queen Anne turrets at its corners, curve-glazed double-hung windows in the turrets and tri-partite windows along the flanks under two round-arched entrances on the west. The design is attributed to the Reid Brothers, James W. and Merritt Reid, but no original drawings and no primary source naming them have been found.

    San Francisco Planning Department, Geneva Office Building and Power House Demolition Project: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 97.345E, 1998.
  4. 1906-1917

    The United Railroads opened the office's second floor as a social hall that could be turned into dormitories for live-in strike-breakers, and cut a door into the east wall so they could reach the yard without passing the strikers at the gate. The carmen struck in 1906, 1907 and 1917.

    San Francisco Planning Department, Geneva Office Building and Power House Demolition Project: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 97.345E, 1998.
  5. April 18, 1906

    The earthquake brought down the whole second-floor wall on the office's north elevation and part of the matching section on the east. The collapsed sections were rebuilt in wood framing and diagonal bracing was added in the window arches.

    San Francisco Planning Department, Geneva Office Building and Power House Demolition Project: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 97.345E, 1998.
  6. May 10, 1906

    The office was back in full use three weeks after the earthquake.

    San Francisco Planning Department, Geneva Office Building and Power House Demolition Project: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 97.345E, 1998.
  7. 1910

    The power house was altered: its multi-paned windows were changed, the central bay's openings widened under shallow segmental arches, and the outer west and south walls encased in cement from the mezzanine up.

    San Francisco Planning Department, Geneva Office Building and Power House Demolition Project: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 97.345E, 1998.
  8. March 1916

    The funeral car service the office had run came to an end — three electric cars fitted with a coffin compartment at the front, a coffin elevator and seats for mourners behind, working the spurs off the electric road to the cemeteries.

    San Francisco Planning Department, Geneva Office Building and Power House Demolition Project: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 97.345E, 1998.
  9. 1928

    The rebuilt walls were wrapped in tar paper.

    San Francisco Planning Department, Geneva Office Building and Power House Demolition Project: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 97.345E, 1998.
  10. 1944

    Transferred to the Municipal Railway (MUNI) in 1944 along with the rest of the Market Street Railway system.

    OMI Neighborhoods Historical Context Statement, adopted 2010
  11. 1956-1979

    The Geneva Avenue yard was the only working streetcar depot in the Bay Area.

    San Francisco Planning Department, Geneva Office Building and Power House Demolition Project: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 97.345E, 1998.
  12. late 1970s

    The car barn beside the office was demolished.

    San Francisco Planning Department, Geneva Office Building and Power House Demolition Project: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 97.345E, 1998.
  13. 1981

    The power house had been abandoned by the time a new power grid was laid in for the Boeing light rail vehicles taking over from the older electric cars.

    San Francisco Planning Department, Geneva Office Building and Power House Demolition Project: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 97.345E, 1998.

  1. 1888

    The Sharon Building went up at 300 Bowling Green Drive as a canteen for women and children, and is now a community art centre. One of the few Richardsonian Romanesque buildings in the city and the only local example with rough-hewn stone walls, it is City Landmark No. 124.

    Victorian Era Styles Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  2. 1889

    The Alvord Lake Bridge was built; it is listed as a National Historic Landmark.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, California Academy of Sciences, Golden Gate Park: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 2002.0782E, 2003.
  3. 1894

    The Fine Arts Building of the California Midwinter International Exposition, designed by the architect C. C. McDougall, was built here — the first of the five building campaigns that made the museum.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, New de Young Museum: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 1999.455E, 2000.
  4. 1894

    The Music Concourse, of almost nine acres, was completed as the Grand Court of Honor for the California Mid-Winter International Exposition, which opened in January 1894.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, California Academy of Sciences, Golden Gate Park: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 2002.0782E, 2003.
  5. November 15, 1910

    A charter amendment passed on this day allowed the California Academy of Sciences to build its first building in Golden Gate Park.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, California Academy of Sciences: [Draft] Environmental Impact Report, 1987.
  6. 1911

    The Academy's trustees retained the architect Lewis P. Hobart, who would set the Beaux-Arts plan the buildings followed for four decades. The site chosen was the former location of the Mid-Winter Fair's Mechanical Arts Building.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, California Academy of Sciences, Golden Gate Park: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 2002.0782E, 2003.
  7. 1914

    Work began on North American Hall, called the West Wing, the first building on the Academy's Golden Gate Park site; it was finished in 1916, an early example of a building type then rare in the West.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, California Academy of Sciences, Golden Gate Park: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 2002.0782E, 2003.
  8. 1917

    The M. H. de Young Memorial Museum was built to the design of Louis Christian Mullgardt, over 1917 to 1921.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, New de Young Museum: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 1999.455E, 2000.
  9. 1923

    Steinhart Aquarium opened, the largest and most complete aquarium in the country at the time and one of the first municipal aquariums in the western United States, designed in the Neo-Classical manner by Lewis P. Hobart after he had toured aquariums in Detroit, Washington, Philadelphia, Boston and Miami.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, California Academy of Sciences, Golden Gate Park: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 2002.0782E, 2003.
  10. 1925

    The museum was expanded by the architects Weeks and Day.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, New de Young Museum: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 1999.455E, 2000.
  11. 1930

    The 1894 Fine Arts Building was demolished in the course of a central expansion and interior remodelling designed by Frederick H. Meyer.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, New de Young Museum: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 1999.455E, 2000.
  12. 1931

    The East Wing was completed. A rendering of Hobart's proposed north elevation, printed in the San Francisco Chronicle on 25 September 1929, had shown a grand central portico linking the two exhibition halls that was never built.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, California Academy of Sciences, Golden Gate Park: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 2002.0782E, 2003.
  13. 1934

    Simson African Hall was built; the 2003 evaluation found it eligible for the California Register in its own right.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, California Academy of Sciences, Golden Gate Park: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 2002.0782E, 2003.
  14. 1935–1941

    Works Progress Administration workers remodelled the police training academy here in Golden Gate Park and graded and paved a parking lot for it. The building is now the Golden Gate Park Senior Center.

    San Francisco New Deal Historic Context Statement, adopted 2023
  15. 1935–1941

    The Works Progress Administration added a one-storey concrete extension to Kezar Pavilion and replaced the stadium's wooden seating with concrete seating for 60,000 spectators.

    San Francisco New Deal Historic Context Statement, adopted 2023
  16. 1935–1941

    The Works Progress Administration began the preliminary planning of the arboretum in Golden Gate Park.

    San Francisco New Deal Historic Context Statement, adopted 2023
  17. 1937

    The Beach Chalet holds a group of 1937 Federal Art Project works: Lucien Labaudt's San Francisco Scenes frescoes and monochrome frescoes, a mosaic by Primo Caredio and magnolia-wood carvings of sea creatures by Michael von Meyer. It is City Landmark No. 179.

    San Francisco New Deal Historic Context Statement, adopted 2023
  18. 1949

    The museum's exterior was remodelled by the City Architect Dodge A. Riedy.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, New de Young Museum: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 1999.455E, 2000.
  19. 1951

    Science Hall was completed to the design of the San Francisco firm Weihe, Frick & Kruse.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, California Academy of Sciences, Golden Gate Park: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 2002.0782E, 2003.
  20. 1952

    Morrison Planetarium, part of Science Hall, was completed — one of the earliest major planetariums in the United States, and found eligible for the California Register in 2003.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, California Academy of Sciences, Golden Gate Park: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 2002.0782E, 2003.
  21. 1964

    The museum's west wing was demolished and the Brundage Wing built in its place, to the design of Gardiner Dailey.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, New de Young Museum: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 1999.455E, 2000.
  22. 1976

    Pflueger designed Wattis Hall, a research and exhibition hall of concrete in the Modernist manner, like Cowell Hall before it.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, California Academy of Sciences, Golden Gate Park: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 2002.0782E, 2003.
  23. 1977

    The Fish Roundabout was completed, the first exhibit of its kind in the country.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, California Academy of Sciences, Golden Gate Park: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 2002.0782E, 2003.

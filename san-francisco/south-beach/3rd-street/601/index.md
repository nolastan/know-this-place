# 601 Third Street

A 1920 bank building at Third and Brannan, inside the South End Historic
District. Published guides mark the site as Jack London's 1876 birthplace —
but the structure he was born in is long gone, and the building here postdates
it by four decades.

> **A commercial building.** This site documents residential addresses first
> and defers business addresses. This page exists because 601 Third Street was
> named in the article that prompted these pages, and because the parcel
> carries a notable site history.

## The building

- Built 1920 (assessor roll and SF Planning both record 1920)
- Commercial, assessor class "Bank"; 2 stories, 21 rooms, 4 baths
- Building area 14,360 sq ft; lot 18,905 sq ft
- Class-B (non-combustible) construction
- Zoning SLI (Service/Light Industrial); Supervisorial District 6

## Historic status

- CEQA Category A / A* — a historical resource within an Article 10 historic
  district
- South End Historic District, period of significance **1867–1935**; **listed**
  on the National Register and the California Register, and an Article 10 local
  district
- This is the only district documented on this site that is listed — not
  merely eligible — on both registers as well as being locally protected

## The site's history

A published guide to notable San Francisco addresses records Jack London as
born on this site in 1876, states that the original building was destroyed, and
notes a small plaque dedicated to him. The building now on the parcel dates from
1920, so it is not the structure London was born in — the guide's claim is about
the ground, not the building. No underlying source is cited.

## Value

The 2025 roll totals $2,978,670 but reports land and improvements at the
identical figure of $1,489,335 each — the total is usable, the split is not.

## Not yet documented

The architect and builder of the 1920 building; the wording and exact location
of the London plaque; confirmation of the birthplace claim against a primary
source; and what stood on the site between 1876 and 1920.

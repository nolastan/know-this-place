  1. 1888

    Station B was built in 1888 to the design of Percy & Hamilton, commissioned by the California Electric Light Company as its second generating plant in the city and sited at Townsend Street and Clarence Place to draw on the water of Mission Bay for its steam boilers. The original three-storey brick building held an office and engine room on the first floor, dynamos on the second and storage on the third, with boilers and smokestack behind; its 150-foot stack was for a time the tallest in the West.

    San Francisco Planning Department, 178 Townsend Street Project: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 2005.0470E, 2007.
  2. 1894

    The plant changed hands twice in the 1890s: the Edison Light & Power Company took it, most likely on buying the California Electric Light Company in 1891, and in 1894 merged with the San Francisco Gas Light Company to form the San Francisco Gas & Electric Company. A new plant went up across Townsend Street between 1903 and 1905.

    San Francisco Planning Department, 178 Townsend Street Project: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 2005.0470E, 2007.
  3. 1908

    The 1906 earthquake collapsed part of the engine room at the front while the rear and the 150-foot stack came through intact. Around 1908 the damaged second and third floors were taken off and the front capped with a stepped gable parapet, the front roof rebuilt in wood framing over galvanised iron; an office was added at the south-east corner and twelve horse stalls put in at the north-west. Between 1899 and 1908 the building was recast as a single-storey high bay on light steel trusses and raised 9 feet 6 inches.

    San Francisco Planning Department, 178 Townsend Street Project: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 2005.0470E, 2007.
  4. 1915

    The building worked as a feed mill from 1908 to 1927, the Producers Hay Company cutting a three-by-nine-foot window and a door of the same size into the brick in 1915 to light and air the office. It stood empty from 1928 into the early Depression years.

    San Francisco Planning Department, 178 Townsend Street Project: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 2005.0470E, 2007.
  5. 1955

    The Charles J. Worth Drayage Company used the building as a general warehouse and drayage yard between 1935 and 1943, widening the office entrance to a double door with a transom in 1934. The San Francisco Warehouse Company bought it in 1955 and widened the vehicle entrance on Townsend Street from twelve feet to fourteen; warehouse use continued to 1967. After two years empty the ship repairers West Winds Inc. moved in and stayed through at least 1995.

    San Francisco Planning Department, 178 Townsend Street Project: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 2005.0470E, 2007.

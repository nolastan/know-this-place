  1. 1882

    Exhibition building moved here

    The Mechanics' Institute moved its exhibition building to this block. The hall ran to nearly 170,000 square feet and could hold more than 10,000 people.

    Taryn Edwards, “Before the Midwinter Fair: The Mechanics' Institute's ‘Pacific Rim' Industrial Exhibitions of 1869 and 1871,” The Argonaut: Journal of the San Francisco Historical Society, vol. 30, no. 1 (Summer 2019), pp. 8–23
  2. 1906

    Exhibition building burns

    The exhibition building survived the earthquake and was used briefly as a hospital, then burned in the fire that followed.

    Taryn Edwards, “Before the Midwinter Fair: The Mechanics' Institute's ‘Pacific Rim' Industrial Exhibitions of 1869 and 1871,” The Argonaut: Journal of the San Francisco Historical Society, vol. 30, no. 1 (Summer 2019), pp. 8–23
  3. 1909-06

    Land leased to the city

    An accord was reached and the land was leased to the City of San Francisco for the development of the civic center.

    Taryn Edwards, “Before the Midwinter Fair: The Mechanics' Institute's ‘Pacific Rim' Industrial Exhibitions of 1869 and 1871,” The Argonaut: Journal of the San Francisco Historical Society, vol. 30, no. 1 (Summer 2019), pp. 8–23
  4. 1912

    City buys the block

    The City of San Francisco bought the land for $700,000, ending the era of the Mechanics' Institute industrial exhibitions.

    Taryn Edwards, “Before the Midwinter Fair: The Mechanics' Institute's ‘Pacific Rim' Industrial Exhibitions of 1869 and 1871,” The Argonaut: Journal of the San Francisco Historical Society, vol. 30, no. 1 (Summer 2019), pp. 8–23
  5. 1933–1934

    The California Civil Works Administration ran its programme from the third floor here, with about eighty clerks administering its projects, and the Municipal Employment Bureau in the same building verified that applicants were unemployed. The Works Progress Administration later rehabilitated the building: general repairs, new plumbing and heating, new flooring, a mezzanine and a remodelling of the third and fourth floors.

    San Francisco New Deal Historic Context Statement, adopted 2023

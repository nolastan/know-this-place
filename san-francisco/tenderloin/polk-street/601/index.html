  1. 1912–1984

    Social center of the German community

    The German House was the social center of San Francisco's German community, housing dozens of clubs and societies.

    Stefanie E. Williams, “The Rise and Decline of the German-Speaking Community in San Francisco, 1850–1924,” The Argonaut: Journal of the San Francisco Historical Society, vol. 31, no. 1 (Summer 2020), pp. 6–51
  2. December 1912

    German House completed

    The German House was completed, following a cornerstone-laying ceremony at Polk and Turk Streets attended by German Consul General Bopp, Mayor James Rolph and Governor Hiram Johnson.

    Stefanie E. Williams, “The Rise and Decline of the German-Speaking Community in San Francisco, 1850–1924,” The Argonaut: Journal of the San Francisco Historical Society, vol. 31, no. 1 (Summer 2020), pp. 6–51
  3. 1960s

    The Club Puertorriqueño de San Francisco held events in California Hall through the 1960s and 1970s.

    Citywide Historic Context Statement: Clubs and Social Halls (1848–1989), 2026
  4. 1965

    California Hall, a designated San Francisco landmark (No. 174); the Daughters of Bilitis held their first public event here in April 1956, and on 1 January 1965 the New Year's Mardi Gras Ball held here for the Council on Religion and the Homosexual was met by police with floodlights and cameras, an episode that turned clergy and lawyers into public allies of the city's homophile organisations.

    Donna J. Graves and Shayne E. Watson, Citywide Historic Context Statement for LGBTQ History in San Francisco, San Francisco Planning Department, adopted by the Historic Preservation Commission November 18, 2015
  5. 1970

    The Metropolitan Community Church, the second-oldest LGBTQ congregation in the United States, held its services at California Hall here in 1970 and 1971.

    Damon Scott for the Friends of 1800, Sexing the City: The Development of Sexual Identity Based Subcultures in San Francisco, 1933–1979, historic context statement, San Francisco Planning Department, adopted 2004

    The building was sold and converted to a culinary academy.

    Stefanie E. Williams, “The Rise and Decline of the German-Speaking Community in San Francisco, 1850–1924,” The Argonaut: Journal of the San Francisco Historical Society, vol. 31, no. 1 (Summer 2020), pp. 6–51

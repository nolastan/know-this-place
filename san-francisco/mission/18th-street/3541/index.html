  1. 1979

    Built in 1910 as a Turnverein hall for the city's German community and later used by Norwegian organisations as Dovre Hall, the building was bought by the San Francisco Women's Centers and opened in autumn 1979 as The Women's Building, the first women-owned and women-operated community centre in the United States. A designated San Francisco landmark (No. 178).

    Donna J. Graves and Shayne E. Watson, Citywide Historic Context Statement for LGBTQ History in San Francisco, San Francisco Planning Department, adopted by the Historic Preservation Commission November 18, 2015

  27. July 20, 2022

    City Landmark No. 178

    Listed as San Francisco Landmark No. 178, Mission Turn Hall (the Women's Building), and — at 3543 18th Street — among the properties in the survey area listed in or formally determined eligible for the National Register and the California Register. Built as the Mission Turn Verein Hall at 18th and Lapidge Streets, a hall for gymnastics that gave association space to San Franciscans of German origin and a home to the German Men's Chorus.

    Mission Dolores Neighborhood Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022

Eureka Valley Historic Context Statement (2017)

Address as surveyed3543 18th Street / 3548 18th Street

Named among the Scandinavian institutions near, but outside, the study area as the Turn Verein/Dovre Hall, now the Women's Building, City Landmark No. 178. The statement prints the address as 3543 18th Street where it records the Eureka Valley Social Club's first ball here in 1904 and as 3548 18th Street in two later passages.

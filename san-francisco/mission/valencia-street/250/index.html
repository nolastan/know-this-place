  1. 1906

    Designed by the San Francisco architect Albert Pissis for Levi Strauss & Co.: a heavy-timber frame, wood-clad, single-use industrial building of a kind rarely built in San Francisco after the earthquake, and possibly the only one of its size. The survey's own appendix dates the building 1907.

Eligible districtMission Reconstruction District; Inner Mission Commercial Corridor

Contributor to the Mission Reconstruction District locally significant area (250 Valencia St). Criterion C: the survey's proposed district for the northern Mission's rebuilding after the 1906 earthquake and fire, period of significance May 1906–1913, predominantly Classical Revival, Edwardian and Mission Revival in wood frame construction. Contributor to the Inner Mission Commercial Corridor locally significant area (250 Valencia). Formerly called the 16th Street Commercial Corridor. Criterion C: San Francisco's largest concentration of residential-over-commercial and small-scale commercial buildings, spanning both pre-1906 and post-disaster construction, dated c. 1870s–1931. Named among the survey's properties of individual significance (Levi Strauss & Co. factory). Listed among the designated resources found in the survey area as Levi Strauss & Co. factory.

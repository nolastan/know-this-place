  1. 1866

    Sister Aloyese of the Cross established a school for girls opposite Mission Dolores, the first in San Francisco, on land from the 1858 federal grant to the Catholic Church. It superseded part of the Mission rancheria, which survives as an archaeological site.

    Mission Dolores Neighborhood Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  2. 1906

    Firefighters dynamited the Notre Dame Academy to keep the fire from jumping Dolores Street and reaching the mission.

    Mission Dolores Neighborhood Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  3. 1907

    Built.

  4. 1907

    Rebuilt to the design of the San Jose architect Theodore Lenzen, nearly an exact duplicate of the earlier building but more than twice its size, ready for over 500 students by the autumn.

    Mission Dolores Neighborhood Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  5. August 12, 1964

    Photographed, showing the Notre Dame Catholic Girls High School.

    San Francisco Subjects Photograph Collection (SFP 162), San Francisco Public Library
  6. 1988

    The historic Notre Dame School building, from a ground-floor office of which CURAS provided health education to LGBTQ Latinos; Project Inform was also listed here from 1988 to 1990.

    Donna J. Graves and Shayne E. Watson, Citywide Historic Context Statement for LGBTQ History in San Francisco, San Francisco Planning Department, adopted by the Historic Preservation Commission November 18, 2015

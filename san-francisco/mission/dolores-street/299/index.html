  1. 1911

    Virginia Fair Vanderbilt gave the Sisters of the Holy Family the money to build a children's day home at the corner of Dolores and 16th Streets; Willis Polk designed the Italianate building.

    Mission Dolores Neighborhood Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  2. 1911

    The three-storey building on the 16th Street corner was built for the Holy Family Day Home, on a building permit issued to Willis Polk and Company. It was the second of the order's three day homes to be rebuilt after the 1906 earthquake and fire destroyed all of them.

    299 Dolores Street DEIR, SF Planning Department, 1998
  3. 1924

    A third floor was added to the north wing, whose lower two floors had gone up with the main building.

    299 Dolores Street DEIR, SF Planning Department, 1998
  4. 1936

    An auditorium was added at the back, on the east side of the building.

Mission Dolores Neighborhood Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022

Identified as the Holy Family Day Home. The statement's list of properties listed in or formally determined eligible for the National Register and the California Register gives the Holy Family Day Home as 229 Dolores Street, a separate parcel on the same block; its bibliography cites a 1991 archival study of the Holy Family Day Home project parcel at 299 Dolores Street.

  1. 1908

    The Adams Building went up to the design of Washington J. Miller — four storeys and a mezzanine, of masonry and steel with a wood-framed interior, clad in white matte terra cotta and detailed in glazed terra cotta, with Gothic-inspired ornament, large square windows and a hanging cornice. It was characteristic of the retail buildings of its moment. Its decorative corner canopy had since been removed and its mezzanine windows altered.

    222 Kearny Street DEIR, SF Dept. of City Planning, 1983
  2. 1979

    The Foundation for San Francisco's Architectural Heritage rated the building here "A" in the 1979 downtown survey it published as Splendid Survivors — the top of its four grades, which it reserved for the buildings it judged individually most important downtown. The Department of City Planning's 1976 Architectural Inventory coded it 3-F1-3.

    333 Bush Street draft initial study, SF Dept. of City Planning, 1981
  3. October 6, 1982

    The Adams Building was named as one of six buildings in the Hallidie Building Block - Retail Historic District, which the Landmarks Preservation Advisory Board recommended to the City Planning Commission for designation as a city historic district. The district was centred on the Hallidie Building at 130 Sutter Street and covered that side of the block; it also lay within the larger Retail-Shopping Historic District that Heritage had identified as eligible for the National Register.

    222 Kearny Street DEIR, SF Dept. of City Planning, 1983

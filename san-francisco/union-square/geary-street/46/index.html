  1. 1906

    Commissioned in 1906 and designed by Bliss and Faville: a three-storey brick box about twenty-five feet tall, a bearing-wall structure with wood joists, its front less formal than its neighbour's, with the street and mezzanine floors as base and a modest parapet above. Both fronts carried Renaissance Revival detail.

    46 Geary Street initial study, SF Planning Dept., 2004
  2. 1907

    Built.

  3. 1907

    A narrow three-storey masonry building, one of a pair raised on a single lot after the fire. The building permit of 17 July 1906 names Meyer and O'Brien as its architects; it was a bearing-wall structure with wood joists, its front a tripartite composition under a tall parapet and projecting cornice, and it has always held shops with offices or flats above. A third floor was added between 1906 and 1917.

    46 Geary Street initial study, SF Planning Dept., 2004
  4. 1956

    Built as the California Savings Bank to designs by the architect Ward Thomas, and photographed new in 1956: an open front, an open-plan interior, opaque spandrel panels, a projecting box overhang, a blade sign and floated lighting ceilings made of 50-foot plastic fixtures fitted with fluorescent tubes.

    Neighborhood Commercial Buildings Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  5. 1956

    The California Savings Bank, designed 1956 by Ward Thomas: an open visual front, open-plan interior, opaque spandrel panels, a projecting box overhang, a blade sign, and "floated lighting ceilings" of fifty-foot plastic fixtures fitted with fluorescent tubes.

    Modern Architecture and Landscape Design Context Statement, adopted 2011
  6. 1956

    In 1956 the architect Ward Thomas combined the two buildings that stood here into one. The first seventy feet of their two masonry party walls came out, replaced by steel columns and beams to give a column-free interior behind the new front. Only the Maiden Lane elevations keep their original material.

    46 Geary Street initial study, SF Planning Dept., 2004

  1. 1922

    The City bought 60 acres from the Spring Valley Water Company for the recreation complex out of which the Zoo grew.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, San Francisco Zoo Infrastructure Replacement: Preliminary Negative Declaration, 1994.
  2. 1924

    Fleishhacker Playfield and Zoological Gardens opened, named for Herbert Fleishhacker, then president of the Park Commission.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, San Francisco Zoo Infrastructure Replacement: Preliminary Negative Declaration, 1994.
  3. 1925

    The Delia Fleishhacker Memorial Building — the Mother's Building — was built just inside the Zoo's main entry as a resting place for mothers and children.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, San Francisco Zoo Infrastructure Replacement: Preliminary Negative Declaration, 1994.
  4. 1929

    The Zoo was founded, its first animals coming from Golden Gate Park, where animals had been exhibited since 1889.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, San Francisco Zoo Infrastructure Replacement: Preliminary Negative Declaration, 1994.
  5. 1930

    The second Zoo — expanded from 23 to 92 acres and laid out by Lewis P. Hobart — was built through the 1930s. The Lion House, the Pachyderm House, Monkey Island, the Bear Grottos and the Aviary survive from that plan.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, San Francisco Zoological Gardens Master Plan: Draft Environmental Impact Report, 1997.
  6. 1935–1941

    The Works Progress Administration built the zoo's paths, underpasses, lakes, paddocks and lagoons and its Aviary, Lion House, Pachyderm House, Monkey Island, Small Mammal House, Bear Pits, stone boundary wall, café and pump house — a zoo designed to replace fences with moats.

    San Francisco New Deal Historic Context Statement, adopted 2023
  7. 1935

    Sixty-eight more acres adjoining the Zoo were bought.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, San Francisco Zoo Infrastructure Replacement: Preliminary Negative Declaration, 1994.
  8. December 31, 1979

    The Mother's Building was listed on the National Register of Historic Places — the only building at the Zoo so recognised.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, San Francisco Zoo Infrastructure Replacement: Preliminary Negative Declaration, 1994.

  • Built 1907

Grant Avenue was formerly Dupont Street. The flats standing here now went up after the congregation had left the site.

  1. 1852

    Nine Black Christians gathered to begin the religious and social-cultural activities of the congregation, on Dupont — now Grant Avenue — between Greenwich and Filbert.

    The Argonaut, vol. 31 no. 2, Winter 2021
  2. 1854

    The First Colored Baptist Church of San Francisco moved into its first church building, on Dupont Street between Greenwich and Filbert. The State of California's historical landmark registration application for the congregation gives the address as 1642–44 Grant Avenue.

    SF Planning landmark designation report, 2017
  3. 1855

    The congregation took the name Third Baptist Church, as the third Baptist communion founded in the city and to be an inclusive church without a racial designation.

    SF Planning landmark designation report, 2017
  4. 1896

    The congregation left this site for a building at Bush and Powell Streets, which the 1906 earthquake and fire destroyed.

    SF Planning landmark designation report, 2017

Every roll year from 2018 through 2025 records this parcel at a 0.1 ownership share, so both figures are a partial interest rather than the whole parcel. They are not charted here for that reason.

Not yet documented: the architect and builder of the 1907 flats, the early residents, the date of the last recorded sale, and what became of the church building itself — whether the flats replaced it or a later structure. Sources also disagree on two dates: the Argonaut has the congregation beginning its activities here in 1852, while SF Planning's landmark designation report has it forming that year at a private residence on Kearny Street and moving into this building in 1854; and the Argonaut puts the first pastor, the Reverend Charles Satchell, in place in 1854 where the report gives 1856.

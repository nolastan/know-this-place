  1. 1951

    The Parkside branch of the San Francisco Public Library opened, designed by Appleton and Wolfard with landscaping by Lawrence Halprin and built to look less like an institution than a house: an open plan around a copper-hooded fireplace and reading lounge, an outdoor patio, Eames and Herman Miller furniture. The pair went on to design the Marina, Ortega, Merced, North Beach, Eureka, Western Addition and Excelsior branches between 1953 and 1966.

    Parkside District Historic Context Statement, adopted 2008
  2. 1951

    The Parkside branch library, 1951, by Appleton & Wolfard — the first of the eight branch libraries the firm designed, and the prototype for the casual, inviting Midcentury Modern library that broke with the Carnegie branches before it.

    Modern Architecture and Landscape Design Context Statement, adopted 2011
  3. 1959

    Possibly the Park and Shop Market, later Andronico’s Market, designed by architect Robert B. Marquis.

    Architecture, Planning & Preservation Professionals: A Collection of Biographies, adopted 2021

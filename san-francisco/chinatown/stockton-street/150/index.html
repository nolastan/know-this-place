  1. 1896

    The Spring Valley Water Company put up its headquarters on this corner, with the City of Paris store on the first two floors and the water company's offices on the four above.

    Neiman-Marcus Department Store: Draft Environmental Impact Report, 1978
  2. 1906

    The building's interior collapsed in the 1906 earthquake and fire; the Geary and Stockton Street facades stood.

    Neiman-Marcus Department Store: Draft Environmental Impact Report, 1978
  3. 1906-1907

    City of Paris department store, designed by Arthur Brown Jr.

    Architecture, Planning & Preservation Professionals: A Collection of Biographies, adopted 2021
  4. 1909

    The interior was rebuilt for a 1909 reopening to designs by John Bakewell and Arthur Brown, Jr., assisted by Louis Bourgeois, who enlarged the rotunda into an ellipse rising through four floors under a skylight and an art glass dome figuring the ship La Ville de Paris.

    Neiman-Marcus Department Store: Draft Environmental Impact Report, 1978
  5. 1909

    An eight-storey building with a partial ninth floor, about 112,000 square feet in all, went up here; it was taken down with the City of Paris building for the Neiman-Marcus store.

    Neiman-Marcus Department Store: Draft Environmental Impact Report, 1978
  6. 1909

    The Clayburgh Building went up on the block's Geary Street frontage, at 125-129 Geary: four storeys of brick with terra-cotta ornament, three piers each topped by a medallion carved with a cowled, bearded face, and Chicago-style windows. Salfield & Kohlberg were the architects. A women's clothing shop, Koenig & Collins, took the ground floor, and lofts above held offices and other trades.

    San Francisco Planning Department, Neiman Marcus Expansion Project: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 98.813E, 1999.
  7. January 13, 1975

    Designated State Historical Landmark No. 876 on 13 January 1975, and added to the National Register of Historic Places ten days later.

    Neiman-Marcus Department Store: Draft Environmental Impact Report, 1978
  8. 1976

    The Clayburgh Building was rated “2” in the city's architectural survey; the state's historic-resources database later carried it as “3S”, appearing eligible for the National Register on its own.

    San Francisco Planning Department, Neiman Marcus Expansion Project: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 98.813E, 1999.

Neiman Marcus by Johnson & Burgee, built after a six-year fight over the City of Paris department store that had stood on the site. The store was lost, but its elliptical rotunda and stained-glass dome were kept inside the new building. The glass curtain wall around the rotunda was replaced sometime before 2008.

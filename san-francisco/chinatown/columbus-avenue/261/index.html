  1. 1907

    A stuccoed brick commercial building, later the home of City Lights Bookstore; the city's survey names Oliver Everett as its architect.

    North Beach Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  2. 1913

    Vitalini Fotografia Italiana occupied the upper floor here, one of at least six Italian-owned studios on the 1913 Sanborn map.

    North Beach Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  3. 1950

    Photographed, showing the City Lights Bookstore.

    San Francisco Subjects Photograph Collection (SFP 162), San Francisco Public Library
  4. 1953

    City Lights Bookstore, founded by Lawrence Ferlinghetti and Peter D. Martin as the nation's first all-paperback bookstore opened here in 1953; the city's survey names it among the places of the North Beach Beat scene.

    North Beach Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  5. 1956

    City Lights Bookstore, whose press published Howl in 1956, leading to a California obscenity trial that the publisher won.

    Donna J. Graves and Shayne E. Watson, Citywide Historic Context Statement for LGBTQ History in San Francisco, San Francisco Planning Department, adopted by the Historic Preservation Commission November 18, 2015
  6. November 1966

    Staff at City Lights Books were arrested here in November 1966 for knowingly possessing obscene matter with intent to sell, over a collection of love poems. The case became what has been described as the longest municipal court trial in the city's history, a decade after the Howl prosecution.

    Donna Graves for San Francisco Heritage, Citywide Historic Context Statement for Counterculture (1965–1975), San Francisco Planning Department, adopted 17 July 2024
  7. 1982

    Recorded in the city's 1982 survey of North Beach, adopted by the Board of Supervisors in 1999, as an individually listed resource. San Francisco City Landmark No. 228.

    North Beach Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022

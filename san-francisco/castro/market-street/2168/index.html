  1. 1907

    Built under a contract of 4 May 1907 for $35,000 by a consortium of Swedish contractors — Andrew Olson, Carlson & Swanson, and Victor Stanquist — to a design by the Swedish immigrant architect August Nordin, and financed by a $40,000 loan from Eric O. Lindholm, a Swedish immigrant gold miner and banker, whom a contemporary Vestkusten article names as E. Lindblom. One of its two retail spaces was leased to the Swedish-American Mercantile Company, a dealer in imported wines and liquors and a delicatessen selling Scandinavian and German specialties.

    Duboce Triangle Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  2. 1907

    Built as the Swedish-American Hall, home of the Swedish Society — founded in 1873 as the Original Orpheus Singing Club, then the Singing Society Svea, then the Svenska Sallakapet — and still the San Francisco Swedish Society's headquarters. The statement gives the hall's address as 2174 Market Street in one chapter and 2168 Market Street in another.

    Market & Octavia Historic Context Statement, adopted 2007

Duboce Triangle Historic Context Statement (2022)

StyleCraftsman / Art Nouveau

City Landmark No. 267, and the last of the six religious and fraternal buildings the statement counts. A fanciful blend of the Craftsman and Art Nouveau styles with ornament nominally derived from traditional Swedish architecture, though the statement judges the result more German or Swiss than Swedish. The interior is almost entirely original: lodge rooms, a banqueting hall, a kitchen, a library, club rooms and a bar, with the Café du Nord rathskeller in the basement. Christopher VerPlanck's context statement of 2022 surveyed 552 properties in Duboce Triangle for the Duboce Triangle Neighborhood Association. The 2017 Eureka Valley context statement lists the hall among the designated landmarks related to Eureka Valley that stand outside its own study area, and among the Swedish, Danish and Norwegian institutions of Upper Market Street. It prints the address as 2168-2173 Market Street in its list of landmarks and as 1274 Market Street in two later passages.

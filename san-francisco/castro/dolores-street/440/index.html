  1. 1898

    The first Mission High School was completed on land formerly owned by the Jewish Cemetery Association — a monumental Renaissance Revival building by Charles I. Havens, and the first public comprehensive high school in San Francisco. A figure caption in the same statement dates it 1897.

    Mission Dolores Neighborhood Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  2. 1906

    The school survived the earthquake and fire and overlooked the refugee camp in Mission Park.

    Mission Dolores Neighborhood Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  3. 1936

    Edith Hamlin, assisted by Betty Wiley and Jay Risling, painted three murals here for the Federal Art Project: two six-by-twenty-four-foot works in the library, Civilization Through the Arts and Crafts as Taught to the Neophyte Indians and Mission San Francisco de Asis, and a third in the stair tower that was removed in 1973. The school is City Landmark No. 255.

    San Francisco New Deal Historic Context Statement, adopted 2023
  4. 1938

    Mission High School had no room for playing fields when John Reid Jr. built it in the 1920s. In 1935 the Board of Education asked the Public Works Administration for $65,000 to lay out fields north of the academic building; the block of Dorland Street between Dolores and Church was closed and about twenty buildings on its north side were condemned and cleared before work began in late 1938 or early 1939.

    San Francisco New Deal Historic Context Statement, adopted 2023
  5. June 22, 1964

    Photographed, showing the Mission High School.

    San Francisco Subjects Photograph Collection (SFP 162), San Francisco Public Library
  6. 1989

    Mission High School, where the 1989 conference Dynamics of Color on race, gender and sexual orientation was held; the school also hosted regional and national conferences of gay and lesbian choruses in the 1980s. A designated San Francisco landmark (No. 255).

    Donna J. Graves and Shayne E. Watson, Citywide Historic Context Statement for LGBTQ History in San Francisco, San Francisco Planning Department, adopted by the Historic Preservation Commission November 18, 2015

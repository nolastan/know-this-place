  1. March 22, 1776

    Site selected

    An expedition sent from Monterey selected the sites of the projected Mission and Presidio.

    John S. Hittell, A History of the City of San Francisco and Incidentally of the State of California (San Francisco: A. L. Bancroft & Company, 1878), sections 12–14, 24 and 27
  2. June 27, 1776

    Founding party arrived

    The train of founders, which had left Monterey on 17 June under Friars Francisco Palou and Benito Cambon with seven married civilian settlers and seventeen married dragoons under Don José Moraga, reached the site of the Mission.

    John S. Hittell, A History of the City of San Francisco and Incidentally of the State of California (San Francisco: A. L. Bancroft & Company, 1878), sections 12–14, 24 and 27
  3. October 8, 1776

    Mission dedicated

    A procession of the whole male population walked from the Presidio bearing the banner of the cross and an image of St. Francis; Palou chanted a mass and preached on the founder of his order, and the soldiers and sailors fired salutes of musketry.

    John S. Hittell, A History of the City of San Francisco and Incidentally of the State of California (San Francisco: A. L. Bancroft & Company, 1878), sections 12–14, 24 and 27
  4. June 1, 1777

    First baptisms

    The first three converts were received into the church on the same day.

    John S. Hittell, A History of the City of San Francisco and Incidentally of the State of California (San Francisco: A. L. Bancroft & Company, 1878), sections 12–14, 24 and 27
  5. 1783

    Palóu moved the mission quadrangle to this site, possibly because the earlier buildings stood on valuable agricultural land, and the foundation of the present church was laid.

    Mission Dolores Neighborhood Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  6. 1788

    Construction of the present church began. Over 30,000 adobe bricks were made during the three years of building, by the forced labour of American Indians at the mission.

    Mission Dolores Neighborhood Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  7. April 2, 1791

    The church was dedicated.

    Mission Dolores Neighborhood Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  8. 1808/1818

    Earthquakes

    The 1808 earthquake shattered the houses at the presidio; in 1812 the church at San Juan Capistrano was thrown down and the buildings at Purisima, Santa Inez, San Buenaventura and Santa Barbara injured; in 1818 Santa Clara suffered enough to require a new church. No damage at this Mission is recorded in any of the three, and no account has been found that its church was ever rebuilt or seriously injured.

    John S. Hittell, A History of the City of San Francisco and Incidentally of the State of California (San Francisco: A. L. Bancroft & Company, 1878), sections 12–14, 24 and 27
  9. 1835

    Secularization

    The friars were deprived of control of the Mission property and Gumecindo Flores was appointed administrator; cultivation was neglected, the Indians left, and there was soon nothing to divide.

    John S. Hittell, A History of the City of San Francisco and Incidentally of the State of California (San Francisco: A. L. Bancroft & Company, 1878), sections 12–14, 24 and 27
  10. 1876

    The St. Francis Catholic Church was completed next to the mission sanctuary — a Gothic Revival brick building with a steeply pitched gable roof and a central bell-tower entry.

    Mission Dolores Neighborhood Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  11. April 18, 1906

    The St. Francis Catholic Church was badly damaged by the earthquake and later demolished; the mission chapel and cemetery escaped the fire, which firefighters stopped at Dolores Street.

    Mission Dolores Neighborhood Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  12. 1913

    Built.

  13. 1913

    The cornerstone of the Mission Dolores Basilica was laid, to an austere Mission-style design by Frank T. Shea and John O. Loftquist. Shea, a Beaux-Arts trained architect known as the church builder of San Francisco, had already designed Holy Cross Church, St. Brigid's, St. Vincent de Paul, Nuestra Señora Guadelupe and St. Paul's; Loftquist served as his engineer.

    Mission Dolores Neighborhood Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  14. 1918

    Willis Polk restored the mission, photographing the roof before removing it, setting a steel framework beneath it and returning the original rafters and their buckskin thongs, reusing the original tiles where he could and tinting new ones to match.

    Mission Dolores Neighborhood Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  15. December 25, 1918

    The first mass was held in the concrete-and-steel basilica.

    Mission Dolores Neighborhood Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022

Here Today (1968)Listed

Listed as Mission Dolores and as San Francisco Landmark No. 1, designated in 1968. The statement gives the landmark's boundaries as covering both the mission of about 1781–1791 and the basilica of about 1918 and as spanning Assessor Block 3566, Lots 1 and 2. The Department recommends the designation report be amended, in consultation with community members, to cover the American Indian community — particularly the Ramaytush Ohlone — before, during and after the Mission era.

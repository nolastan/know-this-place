The site was chosen for its harbor rather than its ground: the Mission was to be the nucleus of a seaport town guarding Spain's dominion here, on a peninsula with little tillable land and few Indigenous inhabitants within ten miles.

Site chronology

  1. 22 Mar 1776

    An expedition sent from Monterey selected the sites of the projected Mission and Presidio.

  2. 27 Jun 1776

    The founding party reached the site — seven married civilian settlers and seventeen married dragoons under Don José Moraga, with Friars Francisco Palou and Benito Cambon, ten days out from Monterey.

  3. 8 Oct 1776

    The Mission was dedicated. A procession of the whole male population walked from the Presidio bearing the banner of the cross and an image of St. Francis; Palou chanted a mass and preached on the founder of his order, and the soldiers and sailors fired salutes of musketry.

  4. 1 Jun 1777

    The first three converts were baptized, all on the same day.

  5. 1808–1818

    Earthquakes shattered the houses at the presidio (1808), threw down the church at San Juan Capistrano and injured the buildings at Purisima, Santa Inez, San Buenaventura and Santa Barbara (1812), and forced a new church at Santa Clara (1818). No damage here is recorded in any of the three.

  6. 1835

    Secularization. The friars were deprived of control of the Mission property and an administrator was appointed; cultivation was neglected, the Indians left, and there was soon nothing to divide.

  1. 1905

    Construction began on a vacant 60-by-120-foot lot for San Francisco's Danish community, whose building fund started with 500 Kroner sent by Queen Louise of Denmark.

    Duboce Triangle Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  2. 1906

    Nearly complete when the earthquake struck, the church came through without much damage and its sanctuary was put straight into use by the Red Cross as a field hospital and a shelter for the injured.

    Duboce Triangle Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  3. April 6, 1906

    Completed as the Ansgar Danish Lutheran Church twelve days before the earthquake; it survived and served as a relief centre. The Danish and Finnish Lutheran parishes merged in the 1960s and it was renamed St. Francis Lutheran Church.

    Market & Octavia Historic Context Statement, adopted 2007
  4. 1965

    Renamed Saint Francis Lutheran Church after the congregation merged with the nearby First Finnish (Gethsemene) Lutheran Church of 50 Belcher Street. The statement dates the merger to 1964 in one passage and 1965 in another.

    Duboce Triangle Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022

Duboce Triangle Historic Context Statement (2022)

StyleDanish Gothic

The first church built in Duboce Triangle, City Landmark No. 39, and one of two city landmarks inside the survey area with the Benedict-Gieling House. Brick, with Gothic arch windows, corbelled brickwork and a Danish-style conical steeple; the exterior has remained essentially unchanged. Neither the original architect nor the builder is known. Christopher VerPlanck's context statement of 2022 surveyed 552 properties in Duboce Triangle for the Duboce Triangle Neighborhood Association. The 2017 Eureka Valley context statement names it among the Swedish, Danish and Norwegian institutions of Upper Market Street, near but outside its own study area.

Not yet documented: the architect and builder, the early tenants and the date of the last recorded sale. The Market & Octavia context statement describes a building of 1906 at this address; the assessor dates the building on this parcel to 1900. Submit an update

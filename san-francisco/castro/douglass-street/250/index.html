  1. 1890

    The owner bought a seventeen-acre parcel near Douglass and Caselli and, dissatisfied with the water available, built a holding basin, pumping engine, storage dam and boiler near the house and ran a rival water company supplying local residents. The Board of Health condemned the wells as unfit for domestic use in 1891, and supply to Eureka Valley and Clarendon Heights ended in 1893.

    Eureka Valley Historic Context Statement, adopted 2017
  2. 1891

    Built 1891–1892 on the 8.65-acre Johnson, Ellis, & Burke subdivision, at 78 by 107 feet more than twice the size of most lots in the neighborhood.

    Corbett Heights Historic Context Statement, adopted 2017
  3. 1896

    Occupied as a hospital, 1896–1909, then converted to apartments; known as the Victor Apartments by 1913 and leased by Standard Oil for employee housing during the Second World War. It was the only apartment building in the neighborhood on the 1914 Sanborn map, with fourteen units.

    Corbett Heights Historic Context Statement, adopted 2017
  4. 1897

    The California Medical College ran the house as the Maclean Hospital and Sanitarium; the College of Physicians and Surgeons of San Francisco took it over in 1901 and ran it as California General Hospital, and by 1906 it was in use as the Jefferson Hospital.

    Eureka Valley Historic Context Statement, adopted 2017
  5. April 18, 1906

    Recently vacated by the Maclean Hospital, the building was pressed back into use as a temporary hospital and relief centre after the earthquake and fire.

    Eureka Valley Historic Context Statement, adopted 2017

The 1968 Here Today survey was adopted by the Board of Supervisors under Resolution No. 268-70, so Planning counts the buildings it lists as individual historic resources. The 1976 city architectural survey rated buildings 0 to 5, 5 highest, and was never adopted as a register of historic resources. The 2017 Eureka Valley context statement lists the house among the five designated San Francisco Landmarks in its study area and names it as the one surviving building there associated with the district's early civic and institutional development. The statement dates the house to 1891 in one passage and says it was constructed in 1892 in another.

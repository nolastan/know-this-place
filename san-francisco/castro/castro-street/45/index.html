Earlier record

  1. 1878

    The German General Benevolent Society opened the German Hospital on this block, a 200-bed wood-frame building in the German Rundbogenstil on a campus of grass, palms and shrubs. A women's wing followed in 1888.

    Duboce Triangle Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  2. 1908

    The original hospital was replaced by a five-storey brick complex facing 14th Street, renamed Franklin Hospital during World War I.

    Duboce Triangle Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  3. 1966

    The board launched a $12 million campaign to rebuild the aging campus with two towers and an office building at the northwest corner of the site.

    Duboce Triangle Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  4. 1968

    The three new buildings were completed and the old Franklin Hospital demolished; the hospital was renamed Franklin Medical Center.

    Duboce Triangle Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  5. 1972

    Renamed Davies Medical Center, for the longtime board member Ralph K. Davies.

    Duboce Triangle Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022
  6. 1980s–1990s

    The medical center played a critical role in the treatment of people with HIV and AIDS.

    Duboce Triangle Historic Context Statement, adopted 2022

Duboce Triangle Historic Context Statement (2022)

StyleBrutalist
Year built as surveyed1968

One of three institutional complexes in the survey area, with SFFD Station No. 6 and McKinley Elementary School. The statement writes that the original architects used the site's slope and heavy planting to hold down the visual weight of very large buildings. Christopher VerPlanck's context statement of 2022 surveyed 552 properties in Duboce Triangle for the Duboce Triangle Neighborhood Association.

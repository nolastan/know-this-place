  1. 1970s

    The Midnight Sun, a cruising bar at 506 Castro Street, opened in the mid-1970s with an elaborate film, video and sound system showing old movies, television shows, cartoons and music videos on several screens. Police looking to harass gay drivers stopped them and asked whether they were going down to the Midnight Sun, ticketing any man who showed recognition of the name.

    Eureka Valley Historic Context Statement, adopted 2017
  2. May 21, 1979

    On the night of the White Night Riots, after Dan White's conviction on two counts of manslaughter, two dozen police officers descended on the Castro, striking passers-by with clubs and attacking those seeking safety in the Elephant Walk bar. The violence led to an FBI investigation of the police department at the request of the US Justice Department's civil rights division.

    Eureka Valley Historic Context Statement, adopted 2017

Eureka Valley Historic Context Statement (2017)

StyleMission Revival

Illustrated with 4109-4111 18th Street as a Mission Revival commercial building of 1918, and named as the site of the Elephant Walk bar, which the citywide LGBTQ context statement identified as potentially eligible for the California Register for its importance in the development of Eureka Valley as an LGBTQ enclave. The statement is reconnaissance-level and says inclusion in it is not a judgement on a building's worthiness for preservation.

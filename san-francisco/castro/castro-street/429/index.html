Earlier record

  1. 1922

    The theatre anchored the northern end of the Castro Street commercial corridor, linking the commercial growth on Market Street to the major intersection of Castro and 18th streets.

    Eureka Valley Historic Context Statement, adopted 2017
  2. 1976

    The theatre was revived as a repertory house catering to camp-attuned audiences. That revival, the founding of the Castro Street Fair in 1974 and the move of gay Halloween festivities from Polk Street and North Beach to the Castro in 1976 together solidified the neighbourhood's gay identity.

    Eureka Valley Historic Context Statement, adopted 2017

StyleSpanish Colonial Revival

Listed among the five designated San Francisco Landmarks in the study area, as Landmark #100, and named as the most prominent extant theatre in the district. The statement prints the address as 429 Castro Street in most places, as 429-431 in a figure caption, and as 479 Castro Street in its recommendations and in a quotation from the citywide LGBTQ context statement.

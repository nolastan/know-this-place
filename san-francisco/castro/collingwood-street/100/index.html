  1. 1939

    The city began buying property on the south end of the block bounded by 18th, Collingwood, Diamond and 19th streets for the district's first park, but the war and a lack of funds and materials delayed the work for more than a decade. A $12 million bond measure for playgrounds and recreation spaces passed in 1947; the city acquired the last property on the block in 1950 and broke ground on the Eureka Valley Playground in June 1951.

    Eureka Valley Historic Context Statement, adopted 2017
  2. June 1956

    The recreation centre was completed at a cost of $556,000, after groundbreaking in 1954 and first approval of plans by the Recreation and Park Department in 1945. The building contained a gymnasium, an auditorium and activity rooms.

    Eureka Valley Historic Context Statement, adopted 2017
  3. 1971

    Three hundred gay men attended a meeting of the Eureka Valley Police-Community Relations Council here to support a candidate for its chair, who was elected. The Alice B. Toklas Memorial Democratic Club also met regularly at the centre.

    Eureka Valley Historic Context Statement, adopted 2017

Eureka Valley Historic Context Statement (2017)

Named among the study area's civic and institutional resources, and put forward as possibly eligible for the California Register for its association with park and recreation space development in the neighbourhood and the local advocacy that produced it. The statement dates the recreation centre 1954 in a figure caption and gives 1951 and 1956 for the playground and centre in its text.

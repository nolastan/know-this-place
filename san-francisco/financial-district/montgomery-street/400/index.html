Samuel Brannan put up one of a pair of Montgomery Street buildings on this site in 1853; the building standing here replaced it.

  1. 1853

    Brannan buildings

    Samuel Brannan erected buildings at 400 and 420 Montgomery Street out of the profits of real-estate investments; they were then considered ornaments of the city and evidence of confidence in its future.

    John S. Hittell, A History of the City of San Francisco and Incidentally of the State of California (San Francisco: A. L. Bancroft & Company, 1878), section 231
  2. 1901

    Built.

  3. 1906

    The Alvinza Hayward Building, designed by the firm Percy & Hamilton.

    Architecture, Planning & Preservation Professionals: A Collection of Biographies, adopted 2021
  4. 1906

    The Kohl Building was the only high-rise in the Financial District to survive, in part, the earthquake and fire.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, 505 Montgomery Street: [Draft] Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 82.463E, 1983.

Four of the 157 filings on this address are shown; the rest are office tenant improvements floor by floor and the mechanical, electrical and fire-alarm permits that accompany them. The assessor's roll gives 1901; this address is recorded as built 1906. Business addresses are usually deferred on this site; this page exists because the address was named in the prompting source.

  1. 1910

    Four storeys and a basement built in 1910, stuccoed so that it looks continuous with 425 Battery Street next door.

    San Francisco Planning Department, Battery Street Hotel: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 2000.613E, 2001.
  2. 1911

    Two storeys and a basement, concrete foundation and brick walls, built in 1911. Splendid Survivors gave it a "C" for contextual importance and the Planning Code listed it as Category V.

    San Francisco Planning Department, Battery Street Hotel: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 2000.613E, 2001.
  3. 1912

    Five storeys and a basement on a concrete foundation, finished in stucco, built in 1912. Its stucco front runs on into 427 Battery Street next door so that the two read as one building. The Planning Code listed it — under the address 410 Clay Street — as a Category V building, the lowest rating, and San Francisco Heritage rated it "D".

    San Francisco Planning Department, Battery Street Hotel: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 2000.613E, 2001.
  4. 1912

    The three buildings on this corner — put up in 1910, 1911 and 1912 — have held a dairy, offices, a cigar shop and a candy shop over their history.

    San Francisco Planning Department, Battery Street Hotel: Draft Environmental Impact Report, Case No. 2000.613E, 2001.

The General Harrison

The storeship General Harrison was built at Newburyport, Massachusetts in 1840 and ended up buried in the made ground of the waterfront, where excavation for this building uncovered it in 2001. Its hull is now a 60-foot artwork in the sidewalk outside — flattened copper ribs on a stainless steel frame.

The published account of the work spells the artist's name both “Topher Delany” and “Topher Delaney”; the city's inventory gives Delaney.

Earlier record

  1. Jan 2025

    The California Historical Society announced its permanent closure and the transfer of its collection to Stanford University, which holds it as the California History Collection.

    KQED, January 2025
  2. 2024

    The Society sold the building, by then vacated, for about $6.7 million to a limited liability company affiliated with the San Francisco Baking Institute.

    KQED, January 2025
  3. 2001

    The California Historical Society sold the parcel's transferable development rights, which limits any future redevelopment to repurposing the existing two-story building rather than building to the site's zoned height.

    SocketSite, August 2020
  4. May 1993

    The California Historical Society purchased the building for $2 million and made it its headquarters, converting the basement under the Mission Street sidewalk into climate-controlled collection storage and adding seismic upgrades, a public research library, exhibition galleries and a bookstore.

    SocketSite, August 2020
  5. 1985

    Hundley Hardware Company's occupancy of the building ended.

    SocketSite, August 2020
  6. 1926–1956

    The San Francisco Builders Exchange occupied the building.

    SocketSite, August 2020
  7. 1922

    Architect Andrew H. Knoll designed the two-story Renaissance Revival building on the site, built for the Phoenix Desk and Chair Company.

    SocketSite, August 2020
  8. Jan 1847

    Acting Alcalde Hyde granted Jasper O'Farrell's petition for a 100-vara lot on the north side of Mission Street, between 100-vara grants to Col. John C. Fremont and Alcalde Washington Bartlett.

    The Argonaut, vol. 32 no. 1, Summer 2021
  9. Jan 1847

    Jasper O'Farrell petitioned Acting Alcalde Hyde for the grant of the 100-vara lot.

    The Argonaut, vol. 32 no. 1, Summer 2021

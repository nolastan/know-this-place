The Banker's Heart

Masayuki Nagare carved Transcendence from 200 tons of black Swedish granite for A. P. Giannini Plaza. The San Francisco Chronicle columnist Herb Caen gave it the name the city has used since. Nagare was born at Nagasaki on 14 February 1923 and died on 7 July 2018.

The tower was the tallest building west of the Mississippi until the Transamerica Pyramid passed it in 1972. In April 2018 the United States Geological Survey listed it among 39 San Francisco high-rises whose welded steel frames could be compromised in a strong earthquake.

The Bank of America World Headquarters, 52 storeys, a collaborative design by Wurster, Bernardi & Emmons, Skidmore, Owings & Merrill and Pietro Belluschi. Its dark polished granite broke with the city's habit of light-coloured buildings, and the exaggerated repetition of its triangular bays and near-invisible windows make it a defining Corporate Late Modernist building. A.P. Giannini Plaza, the corporate plaza designed by Lawrence Halprin at the foot of the Bank of America headquarters, and one of the notable public spaces built downtown before the 1985 Downtown Plan required them. Transcendence, the sculpture by Masayuki Nagare installed on the plaza of the Bank of America headquarters.

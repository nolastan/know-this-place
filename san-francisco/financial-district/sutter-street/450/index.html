  2. 1930s

    An Art Deco office building designed by Timothy Pflueger; the endocrinologist Harry Benjamin, who popularised the term transsexual and argued for medical support rather than psychiatric cures, kept a medical office here during summers from the 1930s to the 1970s. Identified as potentially eligible for landmark designation.

    Donna J. Graves and Shayne E. Watson, Citywide Historic Context Statement for LGBTQ History in San Francisco, San Francisco Planning Department, adopted by the Historic Preservation Commission November 18, 2015

Duboce Triangle Historic Context Statement (2022)

StyleArt Deco

Named as one of the landmarks of the Art Deco style in San Francisco, alongside the San Francisco VA Medical Center at Fort Miley of 1934 — a comparison the statement draws to place the two Art Deco buildings in Duboce Triangle. Christopher VerPlanck's context statement of 2022 surveyed 552 properties in Duboce Triangle for the Duboce Triangle Neighborhood Association.

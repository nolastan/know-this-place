  1. 1932

    Built in 1932 for the Japanese YWCA to a design by Julia Morgan, paid for from within the Japantown community and by the national and San Francisco YWCAs. It held a dormitory for ten, classrooms and meeting rooms, a library and an office, and appears to be the only building in the United States purpose-built by and for Issei women. Because the Alien Land Law barred Issei from owning property, the San Francisco YWCA held the title in trust for the community.

    Japantown Historic Context Statement, May 2009
  2. 1942

    After the forced removal of the city's Japanese American population, the Japanese YWCA here was turned over to the San Francisco YWCA and leased from 1942 to 1959 to the American Friends Service Committee, which ran programmes for the Western Addition's new residents. The organiser of the 1963 March on Washington taught a seven-week course in the building and lodged there.

    African American Citywide Historic Context Statement, adopted 2024
  3. 1945–1946

    After the war the American Friends Service Committee used the building and let returning Japanese American women organise activities and take shelter there. The Japanese YWCA was not re-formed; it joined the Buchanan YMCA instead, making the first combined YWCA/YMCA in the country.

    Japantown Historic Context Statement, May 2009
  4. 1954

    The first Mattachine Society Convention was held here on 15-16 May 1954, when the building was in use as the American Friends Service Committee Center.

    Donna J. Graves and Shayne E. Watson, Citywide Historic Context Statement for LGBTQ History in San Francisco, San Francisco Planning Department, adopted by the Historic Preservation Commission November 18, 2015

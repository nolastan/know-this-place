  1. 1885

    Built as the personal residence of Charles L. Hinkel, one of five brothers who worked as owner-builders in San Francisco from the 1870s, buying land, building houses and selling them.

    280 Divisadero Street Carriage House DEIR, SF Planning Department, 2007
  2. 1895

    The Charles L. Hinkel House, built by Charles L. Hinkel in an unusual Second Empire manner with a prominent mansard roof; San Francisco Landmark #190.

    Architecture, Planning & Preservation Professionals: A Collection of Biographies, adopted 2021
  3. March 1964

    The Junior League of San Francisco's Historic Sites Committee photographed the property.

    Junior League of San Francisco Here Today building research files (SFH 611), San Francisco Public Library
  4. 1981

    The upper floor of the carriage house behind the house had been in residential use since some time before 1964; that use was given up around 1981 and the building stood empty from then on.

    280 Divisadero Street Carriage House DEIR, SF Planning Department, 2007

Eureka Valley Historic Context Statement (2017)

Named as San Francisco Landmark #190 and as the house the builder Charles Hinkel lived in from 1885 to 1891, before he moved to Eureka Valley in conjunction with his building activities there.

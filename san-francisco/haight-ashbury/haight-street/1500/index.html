  2. 1965

    The Mnasidika clothing boutique traded in this storefront from 1965 to 1968; reportedly the first hip clothing store in the neighbourhood and instrumental in the Haight-Ashbury's development as a hippie enclave.

    Donna J. Graves and Shayne E. Watson, Citywide Historic Context Statement for LGBTQ History in San Francisco, San Francisco Planning Department, adopted by the Historic Preservation Commission November 18, 2015
  3. 1965

    The Doolan-Larson Building at the corner of Haight and Ashbury, San Francisco Landmark No. 253 as the Richard P. Doolan / Norman T. Larson Residence and Storefronts, and one of the buildings associated with the counterculture era.

    Donna Graves for San Francisco Heritage, Citywide Historic Context Statement for Counterculture (1965–1975), San Francisco Planning Department, adopted 17 July 2024

Progressive Era & Early Revival Styles Historic Context Statement (2023)

StyleColonial Revival
Year built as surveyed1903
Address as surveyed557 Ashbury Street and 1500-1512 Haight Street

The Richard P. Doolan/Norman T. Larson Residence and Storefronts built in 1903 — an extremely rare Colonial Revival mixed-use building, with Palladian windows carrying projecting shelf mouldings, oval windows and a hipped roof.

  1. 1866

    The Board of Supervisors got the legislature's authority to build and run an alms house and hospital, and set aside eighty acres of City land on the west side of Twin Peaks for it.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, Laguna Honda Hospital Replacement: Draft Environmental Impact Report, 2001.
  2. 1867

    The Alms House opened. The first building was a four-storey Second Empire block with three wings off a central core, built for 400 to 500 patients and overlooking the Spring Valley Water Company's Lake Honda; a separate 40-bed infirmary stood beside it.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, Laguna Honda Hospital Replacement: Draft Environmental Impact Report, 2001.
  3. 1907

    The 340-bed infirmary on the Laguna Honda campus was built over 1907 and 1908, among the first concrete buildings put up after the fire.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, Laguna Honda Hospital Replacement: Draft Environmental Impact Report, 2001.
  4. 1908

    Clarendon Hall was built on the Laguna Honda campus to the design of the San Francisco Bureau of Architecture and Newton Tharp — the oldest surviving building there, Classical Revival in style, and one of the first hospital buildings put up after the earthquake.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, Laguna Honda Hospital Replacement: Draft Environmental Impact Report, 2001.
  5. 1912

    The campus garage was built at Laguna Honda; an addition was later put on its east end.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, Laguna Honda Hospital Replacement: Draft Environmental Impact Report, 2001.
  6. 1926

    The main administrative block and Wings A to G — the oldest part of the Main Hospital Building at Laguna Honda, in the Spanish Revival manner, with five-storey wings running off a central corridor — were built to the design of John Reid, Jr., the City Architect.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, Laguna Honda Hospital Replacement: Draft Environmental Impact Report, 2001.
  7. 1926

    Service buildings went up as the Laguna Honda campus was expanded: the bridge building spanning the valley between the Main Hospital Building and Clarendon Hall, the greenhouse on the north side of the Main Hospital Building, and the laundry.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, Laguna Honda Hospital Replacement: Draft Environmental Impact Report, 2001.
  8. 1928

    Wings K and L were added to the Main Hospital Building at Laguna Honda, extending it eastward up the hill.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, Laguna Honda Hospital Replacement: Draft Environmental Impact Report, 2001.
  9. 1930

    A four-storey addition of 196 beds was built at Laguna Honda hospital, with Splivoch & Splivoch as the builders.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, Laguna Honda Hospital Replacement: Draft Environmental Impact Report, 2001.
  10. 1935–1941

    Works Progress Administration workers reconditioned the wards of the Laguna Honda Home for the Aged and Infirm to improve capacity and comfort. New Deal artists worked on the campus too: Glen Wessels painted five murals in the main lobby — The Professions and Earth, Air, Water and Fire — and David Lindstrom carved a cast-stone Florence Nightingale for the main entrance.

    San Francisco New Deal Historic Context Statement, adopted 2023
  11. 1935

    Wings M and N of the Main Hospital Building at Laguna Honda, now called Wing O, were added.

    San Francisco Department of City Planning, Laguna Honda Hospital Replacement: Draft Environmental Impact Report, 2001.

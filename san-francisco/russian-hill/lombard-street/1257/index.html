  1. 1876

    A flat-front Italianate house built by a carpenter for himself at the rear of the lot, and one of the three best-preserved nineteenth-century buildings on these blocks — essentially unchanged outside. Its French cap, a miniature mansard over the cornice, carries diamond-shaped shingles that repeat on the pent roofs over the door and windows.

    The West Slope of Russian Hill: A Historical Context and Inventory of Historic Resources, 2006, revised 2009
  2. 1900

    Built.

  3. 1904

    Classical Revival flats built at the front of a lot that already held the 1876 cottage behind them, by the architect C. A. Meussdorffer with Ludwig Koenig as contractor. The classical detail is in the arcaded first-story entrance, the casing round the second-story opening, an egg-and-dart course and profiled cornices. Both buildings survive with high integrity.

    The West Slope of Russian Hill: A Historical Context and Inventory of Historic Resources, 2006, revised 2009

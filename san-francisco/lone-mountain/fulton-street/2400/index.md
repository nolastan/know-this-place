# 2400 Fulton Street

A nineteen-room house of 1904 facing Golden Gate Park across Fulton Street,
bought by Jefferson Airplane in 1968 and used as home and workplace for about a
decade.

## The building

- Built 1904 (assessor roll and SF Planning both record 1904)
- 1 unit, 19 rooms, 4 baths, 3 stories; assessor class "Dwelling"
- Building area 8,160 sq ft; lot 7,017 sq ft
- Wood-frame construction (assessor construction type D)
- Zoning RM-1; Supervisorial District 1
- Historic status: CEQA Category A — a historical resource on the strength of
  a survey result. Not a designated landmark; in no historic district

## Notable residents

A published guide to notable San Francisco residences records Jefferson
Airplane and Grace Slick as buying the house in 1968 for around $70,000 and
living and working in it for roughly ten years. The guide describes a
seventeen-room house; the assessor records nineteen rooms. Both figures are
kept here rather than reconciled.

## Permit history

Thirteen permits are on file, 1993–2020; two are street-space filings at a
nominal valuation. The record is dominated by one project: a $125,000
horizontal addition filed in July 1994, three months before the recorded sale,
and not signed off until March 1996 — with a seismic bolt-and-sheathe job and a
partition removal folded into it along the way. A second, much smaller addition
was filed in 1997, renewed twice, and finished only in 2001.

## Ownership and value

The assessor records the most recent sale on **1 November 1994**. The 2025 roll
assesses land at $670,195 and improvements at $793,873 — $1,464,068 total. This
is one of the few addresses documented here where improvements are assessed
above land, which follows from the scale of the mid-1990s work.

## Not yet documented

The architect and builder, the original permit, what the 1994 and 1997
additions actually added, and whether the room count is 17 or 19.

  1. 1935

    Built.

  2. 1936

    Purpose-built premises for the Japanese YMCA, dedicated in 1936: $25,000 raised within Japantown and about $15,000 given by supporters in Japan paid for meeting rooms, a small chapel and a gymnasium.

    Japantown Historic Context Statement, May 2009
  3. 1942

    One of the places in the city where San Francisco's Japanese community was ordered to report for processing in 1942 before wartime removal. The building was let to the USO during the war to serve African American troops.

    Japantown Historic Context Statement, May 2009
  4. 1947

    Returned to the Japanese American community after the war and renamed the Buchanan YMCA; in 1947 it and the Japantown YWCA began a joint inter-racial youth programme for African American and Japanese American children.

    Japantown Historic Context Statement, May 2009
  5. July 1948

    The Buchanan YMCA, which had served mostly Japanese American patrons before the war and became a central facility for Black men and youth after it. The San Francisco Urban League held its first meeting in the U.S.O. office here in March 1944, and in July 1948 more than 300 residents met here to object to the Western Addition redevelopment plan. It was one of three buildings the A-1 plan spared. Identified in 2024 as eligible for local landmark, California Register or National Register designation.

    African American Citywide Historic Context Statement, adopted 2024
  6. July 1948

    More than 300 residents met here in July 1948 to hear the case against the Redevelopment Agency's plans for the neighborhood.

    Japantown Historic Context Statement, May 2009
  7. 1958

    The Redevelopment Agency established its Western Addition Project Office in the building in 1958.

    Japantown Historic Context Statement, May 2009

  3. 1928

    The Westerfeld House, built in 1889 to a design by Henry Geilfuss as a Stick Italianate villa, was rented by the Russian Club from 1928 to 1937. It held the club's library and was the headquarters of the Consolidated Committee of Russian National Organizations, which worked from here on the 1933 amendment to the Registry Act granting amnesty to Russian immigrants; a film was shot inside it in 1933. Émigrés went on renting rooms in the house into the 1950s.

    Nina Bogdan, Russian American Historic Context Statement, San Francisco Planning Department, adopted by the Historic Preservation Commission October 15, 2025

  5. 1966

    The William Westerfeld House, run in the mid-1960s as the communal Russian Embassy house. The underground filmmaker Kenneth Anger lived here from 1966 to 1967 and made several of his important works during that time. It is San Francisco Landmark No. 135 and stands in the Golden Gate Park Historic District.

    Donna Graves for San Francisco Heritage, Citywide Historic Context Statement for Counterculture (1965–1975), San Francisco Planning Department, adopted 17 July 2024

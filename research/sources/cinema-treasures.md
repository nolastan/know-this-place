- **What:** A volunteer-maintained database with one page per movie theatre,
  worldwide. Each San Francisco entry carries the address, the architect and
  firm, the style, the seat count, the screen count, the status, **every
  previous name**, and a curated prose history — and that history is usually a
  dated chronology: the opening date and the film it opened with, the
  remodellings, the renamings, and the date it stopped showing films.
- **Where:** `https://cinematreasures.org/theaters/<id>`. The city index is
  `.../theaters/united-states/california/san-francisco` and it paginates; a
  targeted search for the venue name and street number finds the id faster than
  walking the index. Comments live at `<url>/comments`.
- **Use for:** the one thing no city dataset holds — a theatre's **operating**
  history. The assessor gives a year built and the survey forms give a style;
  neither knows a building opened as the Roosevelt on 22 September 1926 with
  Norma Talmadge in *Kiki*, was renamed twice, and closed as a movie house in
  1993.
- **Why it rates low on search-invisibility, and why we use it anyway:** these
  pages are indexed and rank. The bias in [../AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) prefers
  sources that don't, but it ranks sources rather than excluding them, and for
  a theatre's opening night and closing date there is no search-invisible
  alternative — not in DataSF, not in a nomination, not in a survey form. Reach
  for the primary record first for the *building*; come here for the *theatre*.

### Cautions

- **The overview and the comments are different tiers.** The overview is
  curated and is what to cite. The comments are unmoderated user posts: some
  are from serious historians — Jack Tillmany, whose *Theatres of San
  Francisco* the Uptown Tenderloin nomination cites in its bibliography, posts
  as `tillmany` — and some are reminiscence, advertising or evangelism. Treat a
  comment as a lead to corroborate, not as a fact to publish.
- **The overview carries factual errors, and they are not typos.** The Gateway
  entry (theatre 10339) attributes *Angels in America* to Larry Kramer; it is
  Tony Kushner's play. Nothing on the page flags it. **Check any claim that
  reaches outside the building itself** — a playwright, a star, a studio — before
  republishing it, and drop it if it does not hold up. The dates and the names
  of the theatre are the reliable payload.
- **The address is the theatre's, not always the parcel's.** Cinema Treasures
  gives Brava as 2789 24th Street where EAS puts the parcel at 2761–2791.
  Resolve on the parcel as usual; the number in the header is only a starting
  point.
- **The style field disagrees with the DPR forms often enough to expect it.**
  Cinema Treasures calls Brava's building Mission Revival; the Eastern
  Neighborhoods survey form calls it Spanish Colonial Revival with Moorish
  elements. Record both and put the disagreement in the page's `unknowns` —
  adjudicating is not research.
- **Seat counts are a moment, not a constant.** The header shows the seat count
  *now*; the history usually gives the original. Both are facts and they are
  different facts — say which is which, or don't publish either.

### Why not a corpus pass

The San Francisco index runs to a few hundred theatres, most of them long
demolished, and a pass over it would be a pass over addresses rather than over
a document — the entries are already one-per-building and already resolved by
address. Consult it per address, the way `local-news` is consulted. If that
ever changes, the natural batch is one neighbourhood of the city index.

- **Citation label:** name the theatre's entry and link it — "Cinema Treasures,
  entry for <theatre name>".

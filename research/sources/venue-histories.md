# venue-histories — Venues' own published histories (secondary)

> - **Coverage:** Consulted per address. One page per venue; there is no corpus.
> - **Local corpus:** —
>
> Update this dossier at the end of every pass — the `Verified:` line, the
> coverage note, and anything the pass learned about getting at the source.

- **What:** The "History" or "About" page a theatre, music venue, hotel or arts
  organisation publishes about its own building. Usually a dated narrative: when
  the organisation acquired the building, what it cost, who designed the
  renovation, when it reopened, and what the building was before.
- **Where:** the venue's own domain — `brava.org/history`,
  `presidiotheatre.org/history`, `counterpulse.org/80turk`,
  `fairmont-san-francisco.com` (which publishes a dated *Moments in History*
  PDF). Find it with a search for the venue name plus "history"; it is rarely
  linked from the front page.
- **Use for:** the recent half of a building's life, which the archives do not
  reach. A nomination written in 2008 and a survey form recorded in 2007 both
  stop before the renovation that made the building what it is now. The venue's
  own page is frequently the only record of a 2014 purchase, a 2016 reopening,
  or a closing night.

### Cautions

- **It is an interested party writing about itself.** Expect the dates of its
  own achievements to be exact and everything before its tenure to be loose
  ("a former vaudeville/movie house built in 1926"). Take the organisation's
  own chronology at face value; corroborate anything it says about the
  building's earlier life against a record that was written at the time.
- **Expect it to disagree with the specialist databases, and record the
  disagreement.** Brava's own history says the building was renamed Brava
  Theater Center in 1996 and renovated into a 360-seat main theatre and a
  60-seat studio; Cinema Treasures says it was restored and reopened in the
  autumn of 2001 and shows 450 seats. Both go in the page's `unknowns`.
- **Privacy is the constraint that bites hardest here.** These pages are mostly
  *about people* — the founder, the artistic directors, the executive director,
  the donors, the board. The root [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) bars naming
  current occupants, and an organisation's present staff are exactly that.
  **Take the building's chronology and the named design firms; leave the
  people.** A renovation credited to Jensen Architects is publishable; the
  director who commissioned it is not. Where a donor fund is named in the
  building's own record ("the Margaret E. Haas Fund"), that is an institution,
  not a resident.
- **These pages are rewritten without notice and carry no dates.** There is no
  edition, no revision line and no archive link. **The retrieval date in the
  citation is the only thing anchoring the claim**, so record it, and treat a
  re-check as cheap.
- **Marketing prose is not a fact.** "A San Francisco treasure", "world-class
  artists" and the list of everyone who has ever played the room are not
  citable and would breach the site's no-editorial-voice rule anyway. Take
  dates, names of buildings, and named firms.

- **Citation label:** name the organisation and the page, and link it — "Brava
  for Women in the Arts, 'History of Brava'".
- **Verified:** 2026-09-09 (first use. Four venues of issue #302 publish a
  usable dated history: Brava, the Presidio Theatre, CounterPulse and the
  Fairmont. The Chapel's own coverage is a radio feature about the building
  being haunted and carries no dated building fact; the remaining #302 venues
  either have no history page or publish only a mission statement.)
